What Policies Usually Cover

Sudden and accidental water damage is covered under the majority of standard homeowner's policies. That category takes in failed appliances like water heaters, dishwashers, and washing machines, along with sudden HVAC failures, accidental tub or sink overflows, ice dams, and burst pipes. If you cannot stay in the house, temporary living expenses are typically included, as are water removal, structural dry-out, replacement materials, and the remaining restoration costs.

What's Typically NOT Covered

Several kinds of loss are normally left out: groundwater seepage at the foundation, long-term leaks, problems caused by putting off maintenance, floods from external sources (a separate flood policy is needed), and sewer backups (a specific endorsement is needed). Understanding these exclusions before a loss occurs lets you add endorsements or separate policies to fill coverage gaps.

Steps to File a Successful Claim

To keep your claim protected, begin by documenting the damage thoroughly, with pictures and video, while nothing has been cleaned up yet. Contact your insurance company promptly to report the loss. Take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage, which insurers call mitigation. Hold on to ruined belongings and materials so the adjuster can inspect them first. Have the work done by professionals who are IICRC-certified. Living expenses, temporary repairs, and any other cost connected to the loss should each have a saved receipt. When the adjuster visits, be there to walk them through all of the damage.
