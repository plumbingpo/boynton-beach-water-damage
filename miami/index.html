Simple Ways to Keep a Miami Home Dry

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Miami, and they are worth learning. Paint that bubbles or peels, a brown ring on a ceiling, baseboards pulling away from the wall, and flooring that cups or lifts all mean moisture behind the surface. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

No amount of maintenance prevents every leak. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Miami home full of drying machines for most of a week.

Restoration Challenges Miami Homeowners Face

In Miami, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. If you can spot these early, you have a good chance of stopping the damage before it spreads.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers all depend on small hoses that wear out. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. We see many of these in Miami homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many mold jobs we handle in Miami began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
